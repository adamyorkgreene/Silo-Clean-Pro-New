Routine inspections keep silos safe, efficient, and compliant. Beyond a quick walk‑around, a good inspection follows a consistent checklist and documents what you see. Here’s a field‑ tested guide to the seven checkpoints our teams pay attention to—and what to look for at each stop.

1) Roof and Vents

Scan roof sheets for loose or missing fasteners, cracked sealant, ponding water, hail or impact damage, and corrosion around penetrations. Verify vents are clear, screens are intact, and weather caps operate freely so the bin can breathe without inviting pests or moisture.

2) Wall Seams and Fasteners

Follow vertical and horizontal seams panel‑by‑panel. Look for streaking (a corrosion tell), slipped or buckled sheets, missing washers, ovalized holes, or gasket squeeze‑out. Use a torque procedure where required—random spot‑checks help catch early movement before it grows into a leak path.

3) Foundation and Base Ring

Settlement, cracking, or spalling at the slab, uneven grout under the base ring, and standing water are priority findings. Confirm anchor bolts, nuts, and washers are present and sound; light surface rust is common—active scaling is not. Check for gaps that could admit water or pests.

4) Ladders, Cages, and Platforms

Verify rung integrity, cage condition, guardrails, toe boards, and mid‑rails. Confirm gates and self‑closing hardware function properly. Inspect lifeline anchor points and fall‑arrest components for wear. If something wouldn’t pass a climb test, tag it out and remedy before use.

5) Moisture Ingress and Corrosion

Water leaves clues: staining, caked fines, mold, and rust blooms. Trace streaks upward to their source—often a roof penetration, seam, or failed sealant. Note coating damage and underfilm corrosion early; timely surface prep and recoat is far cheaper than panel replacement.

6) Conveyance and Appurtenances

Examine spouts, distributors, spinners, transitions, and bearings for alignment, wear, and dust leakage. Check drive guards and belt condition. Poorly sealed transfer points create combustible dust accumulations and product loss—both avoidable with small fixes.

7) Safety Signage and Access Control

Confirm confined‑space and lockout/tagout signage is posted and legible. Test access doors and hatches for proper latching and gasket compression. Replace missing tags and update contact info so responders and crews have what they need at a glance.

Quick Inspection Checklist

  • Document date, conditions, and who performed the check
  • Photograph findings with location notes (panel row/column, elevation)
  • Prioritize: safety hazards → active leaks → developing defects
  • Create work orders with materials, tools, and access needs
  • Schedule re‑inspection dates for watched items

When to Call a Pro

If you see structural deformation, suspected underfilm corrosion, entry‑required build‑up, or any condition involving confined‑space work, bring in a qualified team. Professional inspectors and cleaners can verify root causes, remediate safely, and help you build a preventative plan that reduces downtime and extends service life.
